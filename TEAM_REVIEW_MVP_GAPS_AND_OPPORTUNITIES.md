# Orange Business Innovation Radar — Team Review, MVP Gaps, and Development Opportunities

## Purpose

This document gives the team a shared assessment of the work completed so far, the strongest components, the methodological and statistical weaknesses, and the minimum work required for a credible MVP.

It reviews both:

- the research, collection, methodology, and data-profiling work in the current workspace;
- the Streamlit/SQLite application in the `DemoTest` branch of the teammate repository.

The following artifacts were generated by the teammate and are treated as part of the proposed analytical workflow:

- `prompt_triage_rss_orange_radar.md`
- `prompt_scoring_orange_radar.md`
- `orange-innovation-radar-watchlist.html`

The objective is not to choose one implementation over the other. The strongest MVP will combine the existing application foundation with the stricter evidence and scoring methodology.

---

## 1. Target Outcome

The final product should identify specific opportunity spaces for Orange Business and make them visually understandable.

An opportunity space is:

`Vertical × Use Case × Technology`

Example:

`Banking × secure WAN threat visibility × network-as-a-sensor security analytics`

Every published opportunity should answer four separate questions:

1. Is there credible external demand?
2. Why is the opportunity urgent now, next, or later?
3. Does Orange Business have a credible right to win?
4. How confident are we in the conclusion?

The radar must not produce unexplained numbers. Every result must remain traceable to dated evidence, source URLs, factor calculations, and visible evidence gaps.

## 2. Strong Parts Already Completed

### 2.1 Clear opportunity-space definition

The project correctly avoids generic themes such as `AI`, `cloud`, or `cybersecurity`. It uses the more actionable structure:

`Vertical × Use Case × Technology`

This makes results more useful for strategy, sales, presales, workshops, partner assessments, and account development.

### 2.2 Separation of attractiveness and right to win

The methodology distinguishes:

- **Opportunity attractiveness:** whether external demand and timing justify attention;
- **Right to win:** whether Orange Business has the offers, customer access, references, skills, assets, and partners needed to compete.

This is essential. A growing market is not automatically an Orange opportunity, and a familiar Orange capability is not automatically supported by market demand.

### 2.3 Explainable scoring framework

The attractiveness model has a clear weighted structure:

`30% market signal + 20% source diversity + 20% evidence quality + 15% momentum + 15% strategic relevance`

The right-to-win model is also explicit:

`30% offering fit + 25% customer overlap + 25% references + 20% partner readiness`

The deterministic arithmetic implemented in Python is a strong foundation because it prevents calculation mistakes once the factor inputs are valid.

### 2.4 Strong Stage 1 triage prompt

`prompt_triage_rss_orange_radar.md` contains several valuable controls:

- explicit Orange Business strategic pillars;
- a clear relevance test combining strategic fit and evidence value;
- detailed exclusion rules;
- strict treatment of vendor promotion and routine cybersecurity noise;
- support for partial vertical/use-case/technology information at triage stage;
- a short rationale required for every classification;
- a preference for precision rather than retaining every vaguely related article.

This is considerably stronger than keyword filtering alone.

### 2.5 Strong Stage 2 clustering and scoring prompt

`prompt_scoring_orange_radar.md` correctly requires:

- clustering across multiple articles before scoring;
- specific opportunity spaces rather than broad themes;
- mapping to the six initial backlog hypotheses;
- residual handling instead of silently discarding articles;
- evidence and reasoning beside every sub-score;
- conservative scores when evidence is missing;
- provisional treatment of right to win;
- low-confidence topics kept out of the ranked table;
- exactly one practical next action per topic.

The final consistency checklist is particularly useful.

### 2.6 Useful watchlist visualization

`orange-innovation-radar-watchlist.html` provides a strong visual concept:

- urgency encoded as Now, Next, and Later rings;
- bubble size used for attractiveness;
- a summary table;
- detailed topic cards;
- visible confidence labels;
- explicit research gaps;
- refusal to present low-confidence candidates as publishable rankings.

The choice to display zero ranked opportunities when the evidence is insufficient is a methodological strength, not a failure.

### 2.7 Strong application foundation in the DemoTest branch

The teammate implementation provides useful production-oriented components:

- Streamlit interface;
- SQLite persistence;
- source management;
- article ingestion and exact GUID deduplication;
- API batching and request limits;
- retries and error recording;
- run history and progress events;
- company profiles and reference-document extraction;
- stored evidence URLs;
- deterministic score arithmetic;
- deterministic horizon assignment;
- Radar/Watchlist publication gates;
- configuration files for sources, prompts, and weights.

These components can serve as the application shell for the stricter analytical pipeline.

### 2.8 Improved local source metadata

The current `source_registry.csv` contains fields that are important for evidence quality:

- source category;
- default source quality;
- independence group;
- domain;
- active status.

The `source_backlog.csv` also distinguishes authoritative sources that require APIs, portals, newsletters, reports, or manual collection instead of pretending that everything is an RSS feed.

### 2.9 Initial data-quality profiling

The current statistical work detects:

- the number of articles, sources, domains, and independence groups;
- missing content;
- duplicate titles, GUIDs, and URLs;
- old and future-dated records;
- source/category/quality distributions;
- domain and publisher concentration;
- 30-, 90-, and 180-day coverage.

This is necessary before any scoring result can be trusted.

## 3. Current Weaknesses

### 3.1 Raw article volume is still limited and imbalanced

The current 180-day analysis contains approximately 550 valid articles. The main concern is not only the count but the composition:

- industry media represents nearly 88% of the records;
- cybersecurity and enterprise technology represent nearly half of the corpus;
- regulation, procurement, standards, analyst evidence, and named customer proof are underrepresented;
- several target verticals have only a small number of articles.

This can make media-active technologies look more attractive than less-covered opportunities with stronger buyer or regulatory triggers.

### 3.2 RSS cannot provide a reliable historical backfill by itself

Most RSS feeds expose only their latest entries. A 180-day filter can remove old or future records, but it cannot retrieve articles that publishers no longer expose.

The pipeline needs additional collection methods for a credible historical corpus:

- APIs;
- procurement portals;
- publication databases;
- sitemaps and archive pages;
- newsletters;
- selected manual research;
- Orange-owned case-study and partner libraries.

### 3.3 Feed summaries are often insufficient evidence

Many RSS records contain only a headline or short summary. Scoring regulation, buyer activity, outcomes, and technology maturity from these excerpts creates a high risk of unsupported conclusions.

Where permitted, the pipeline should fetch and store readable canonical article content separately from immutable RSS metadata.

### 3.4 Stage separation is not enforced in the application code

The DemoTest pipeline currently asks one model operation to:

- classify relevance;
- produce a complete opportunity triple;
- assign attractiveness factors;
- assign right-to-win factors;
- generate narratives and actions.

This conflicts with the stronger two-stage prompt design. Relevance classification should happen first. Clustering and scoring should occur only after multiple structured evidence records are available.

### 3.5 Per-article scoring cannot measure cluster-level factors

An individual article cannot reliably determine:

- source diversity across a cluster;
- momentum relative to a baseline;
- repeated independent demand;
- right-to-win evidence across Orange sources;
- event-level corroboration.

These values must be calculated after evidence aggregation.

### 3.6 Current cluster matching is too exact

The teammate code creates a slug from model-generated vertical, use case, and technology text. Only exact normalized wording joins an existing opportunity.

This can split one opportunity into several records:

- `private 5G` versus `private wireless`;
- `GenAI` versus `generative AI`;
- `secure access service edge` versus `SASE`;
- singular/plural and wording variations in use cases.

A controlled taxonomy and synonym map are required.

### 3.7 Existing cluster scores can be overwritten by the latest article

The current application counts accumulated evidence but recalculates factors using the newest article's model response. The opportunity record is then updated.

This creates a statistical inconsistency:

`Evidence count = all cluster evidence`

but

`Factor values = primarily the latest article`

Scores must instead be recomputed from all deduplicated evidence records using deterministic aggregation rules.

### 3.8 Source independence is approximated by hostname

Different hostnames do not necessarily mean independent evidence. Related publications, syndicated reporting, and copied press releases may describe the same event.

The local `independence_group` field is a better basis, but event-level deduplication is still required.

### 3.9 Right-to-win values are currently too easy to invent

External RSS articles rarely prove Orange Business offering fit, customer overlap, references, or partner readiness. Requiring numeric values for every right-to-win factor encourages unsupported estimates.

Missing factors should remain:

`N/A — pending Orange evidence`

The UI should show both the provisional score and evidence coverage, for example:

`Right to win: 70/100 provisional — 1 of 4 factors evidenced`

### 3.10 Current publication gates are too permissive

The DemoTest configuration can promote an opportunity with two evidence records, two hostnames, and confidence above 45. Two related vendor articles could therefore pass.

A stronger publishable-topic threshold is:

- at least three independent evidence records;
- at least one buying, regulation, or proof signal;
- no duplicate real-world event inflation;
- source quality and independence verified;
- right-to-win evidence shown separately;
- human review completed.

### 3.11 The HTML contains manually embedded results

The watchlist HTML hard-codes the opportunity data in JavaScript. It does not read a generated evidence or scoring dataset.

This creates a risk that:

- HTML scores differ from the scoring output;
- evidence changes but the visualization does not;
- formulas and displayed totals drift apart;
- URLs and GUIDs are omitted.

The visualization should read a generated JSON file or a database/API response.

### 3.12 Traceability is incomplete in the prompts and HTML

The triage output schema does not currently preserve the article `link`, although Stage 2 requires source URLs.

The schema should include:

`guid,title,source,link,published,classification,...`

The HTML evidence list should expose:

- evidence ID or GUID;
- publication date;
- signal type;
- claim;
- clickable canonical source URL;
- independence group;
- source-quality classification.

## 4. Statistical Accuracy Issues

### 4.1 A target of 1,000 articles is useful but not sufficient

At least 1,000 valid raw articles over 180 days is a reasonable calibration target. It can support prompt evaluation, taxonomy discovery, clustering tests, and initial trend comparisons.

However, 1,000 is not a statistical guarantee. A duplicated or highly concentrated corpus can be less informative than a smaller balanced corpus.

The project should measure:

- unique events, not only articles;
- independent ownership groups, not only sources;
- source-category diversity;
- domain and vertical balance;
- percentage of primary evidence;
- percentage of records with full text;
- percentage of records with valid dates;
- triage precision and reviewer agreement.

### 4.2 Article counts are not independent observations

Several articles may repeat the same:

- press release;
- regulatory announcement;
- customer deployment;
- acquisition;
- product launch;
- analyst forecast.

Counting all of them as separate signals inflates market strength and confidence. Evidence records should be assigned to a shared `event_id`, and the event should count once for signal strength while supporting URLs remain attached as corroboration.

### 4.3 Publisher concentration creates sampling bias

The corpus currently contains substantial concentration in a few publisher families. High article volume may therefore measure editorial output rather than market importance.

Recommended concentration statistics include:

- percentage of records from the top source;
- percentage from the top five sources;
- percentage from the top independence group;
- Herfindahl-Hirschman Index or a simpler concentration ratio;
- effective number of independent sources;
- distribution by source category and vertical.

### 4.4 Source quality should not be an unconstrained AI score

Source quality should start with registry-defined rules and then be adjusted only through transparent evidence characteristics.

Possible inputs:

- primary versus secondary source;
- named buyer, deployment, or regulation;
- measurable outcome;
- direct source URL;
- publication date and event date;
- corroboration by independent evidence;
- human verification status.

### 4.5 Source diversity should be deterministic

The source-diversity factor should be calculated from stored records, not guessed by the extraction model.

It should consider:

- number of independence groups;
- number of source categories;
- number of signal types;
- primary/secondary source balance;
- whether evidence describes distinct events.

### 4.6 Momentum needs a baseline

Recent article volume alone is not momentum.

Momentum should compare:

- a current period, such as the latest 30 or 90 days;
- a baseline period, such as the previous 90 days or previous 12 months;
- equivalent source categories and collection coverage.

If historical coverage is missing, momentum must be displayed as `not yet measurable`, not replaced by an AI estimate.

### 4.7 Date errors can distort every time-based metric

The dataset has contained old and future-dated records. The analysis must exclude or quarantine:

- future publication dates;
- invalid dates;
- unusually old feed entries;
- negative collection delays;
- records where update date is mistaken for publication date.

The raw record should remain preserved, but it should not influence 30-, 90-, or 180-day calculations until reviewed.

### 4.8 Triage accuracy needs measurement

The triage agent should be evaluated against a human-labelled validation sample.

Recommended metrics:

- precision for `RELEVANT`;
- recall for high-value regulation, buying, and proof signals;
- confusion matrix;
- agreement between two human reviewers;
- false-positive rate by source category;
- false-negative rate by signal type;
- percentage of `LOW` confidence items requiring review.

The project prioritizes high precision, but missing a regulation or tender is more costly than missing generic market commentary. Evaluation should therefore be weighted by signal importance.

### 4.9 Score uncertainty should be visible

A single score such as `72/100` can imply more precision than the evidence supports.

Each opportunity should also show:

- evidence count;
- independent event count;
- independence-group count;
- source-category count;
- factor coverage;
- confidence label;
- last refresh date;
- unresolved evidence gaps.

### 4.10 Current HTML contains arithmetic inconsistencies

Some displayed attractiveness totals do not equal the weighted result of their displayed factor values. Final totals should always be calculated in code from the stored factors and never manually duplicated in the visualization.

## 5. Missing Parts Required for the MVP

### MVP requirement 1 — Multi-method source registry

The source model should support:

- RSS/Atom;
- API;
- portal/manual collection;
- sitemap/archive;
- uploaded or linked Orange reference documents.

Required metadata:

- source name and URL;
- source category;
- default quality;
- independence group;
- domain and vertical coverage;
- geography;
- collection method;
- verification status;
- active status;
- last successful collection;
- terms/access restrictions.

### MVP requirement 2 — Reliable 180-day corpus

The collection layer should:

- enforce the analysis window;
- preserve raw records immutably;
- quarantine future and invalid dates;
- support historical backfill beyond RSS;
- record fetch failures and feed health;
- retain canonical URLs and GUIDs;
- fetch full readable content where allowed.

### MVP requirement 3 — Controlled taxonomy

Create reviewed dictionaries for:

- verticals;
- use cases;
- technologies;
- strategic pillars;
- signal types;
- synonyms and aliases.

AI may propose new terms, but human approval should be required before adding canonical labels.

### MVP requirement 4 — Persisted Stage 1 triage

Store one classification per article with:

- article/GUID and URL;
- classification and confidence;
- signal type;
- strategic pillar;
- partial vertical/use case/technology tags;
- rationale;
- prompt version and model;
- review status;
- processing timestamp.

### MVP requirement 5 — Evidence-record layer

Each relevant article should produce one or more distinct, source-supported claims. Every claim needs:

- evidence ID;
- article ID;
- claim;
- supporting quotation or excerpt;
- signal type;
- canonical tags;
- named organizations;
- geography;
- source quality;
- source URL;
- publication and event dates;
- review state.

### MVP requirement 6 — Event deduplication

Group repeated coverage into one real-world event using:

1. canonical URL and GUID;
2. normalized title;
3. named organization and event date;
4. claim similarity;
5. human review for uncertain clusters.

### MVP requirement 7 — Opportunity clustering

Cluster reviewed evidence records by canonical:

`Vertical × Use Case × Technology`

The system should retain:

- backlog match or new candidate status;
- supporting evidence and event IDs;
- residual/unclustered items;
- clustering rationale;
- taxonomy version;
- human merge/split decisions.

### MVP requirement 8 — Deterministic aggregation and scoring

Python should calculate:

- source diversity;
- evidence quality summaries;
- specificity;
- recency;
- momentum where a baseline exists;
- evidence thresholds;
- final weighted totals;
- urgency horizon;
- confidence and publication gates.

AI can extract facts and propose rationales, but it should not directly publish the final score.

### MVP requirement 9 — Separate Orange right-to-win dataset

Build structured records for:

- Orange offerings and assets;
- customer references;
- customer/account overlap;
- partner activities;
- accreditations and delivery roles;
- skills and operational readiness;
- measurable outcomes.

Right to win should be joined to external opportunities using canonical vertical, use case, and technology tags.

### MVP requirement 10 — Dynamic visualization

The radar should consume generated data from SQLite, JSON, or an API. It should not contain manually embedded scoring results.

Every card should display:

- attractiveness and factor breakdown;
- right to win and evidence coverage;
- urgency;
- confidence;
- dated evidence with clickable URLs;
- why hot now;
- why it matters;
- recommended next action;
- unresolved evidence gaps;
- last review and refresh date.

### MVP requirement 11 — Human review workflow

The application needs review controls for:

- relevant/irrelevant triage decisions;
- evidence claims and quotations;
- taxonomy additions;
- event merges;
- opportunity merges and splits;
- factor inputs;
- publication approval.

### MVP requirement 12 — Evaluation and tests

Add tests for:

- date filtering and future-date rejection;
- source ownership and independence grouping;
- taxonomy normalization;
- evidence/event deduplication;
- cluster aggregation;
- weighted-score arithmetic;
- missing right-to-win factors;
- publication gates;
- prompt-schema validation;
- HTML/JSON consistency;
- database migrations.

## 6. Opportunities We Can Still Develop

### 6.1 Procurement-driven opportunity discovery

Add TED and national procurement portals. Procurement notices can provide higher-value buying signals than general news and can identify:

- buyer organizations;
- industries;
- contract scope;
- geography;
- budget where disclosed;
- submission deadlines;
- relevant technologies and requirements.

### 6.2 Regulation-to-commercial-play mapping

Connect regulations and standards to:

- affected verticals;
- compliance deadlines;
- operational use cases;
- Orange offers and partners;
- target-account workshops.

This can turn regulation into an actionable sales or innovation trigger.

### 6.3 Customer and partner knowledge graph

Create relationships among:

- opportunity spaces;
- customers;
- partners;
- Orange offerings;
- deployments;
- regulations;
- evidence events.

This would make right-to-win reasoning more transparent and reusable.

### 6.4 Gap-led opportunities

The radar should identify not only spaces Orange can already deliver, but also high-attractiveness spaces where Orange lacks:

- a partner;
- a reference customer;
- a required skill;
- a packaged offer;
- geographic delivery capability.

These become partnership, acquisition, capability-building, or experimentation opportunities.

### 6.5 Account-specific views

Once customer overlap is available, the same evidence can produce:

- vertical radar views;
- country views;
- named-account opportunity lists;
- partner-specific plays;
- sales-openers and workshop recommendations.

### 6.6 Trend-change alerts

After enough historical data is accumulated, generate alerts for:

- sudden growth in independent evidence;
- a new regulation or compliance deadline;
- a first named buyer or tender;
- transition from pilot to production;
- emergence of an Orange reference case;
- a material partner or competitor move.

### 6.7 Evidence-gap recommendations

For each watchlist candidate, the system can recommend the next most valuable research action, such as:

- find an official regulation;
- locate one independent customer deployment;
- search procurement notices;
- validate an Orange reference;
- confirm partner delivery readiness;
- schedule a refresh after a known deadline.

### 6.8 Reproducible score versions

Store:

- scoring configuration version;
- taxonomy version;
- prompt version;
- model/provider version;
- evidence snapshot;
- calculation timestamp.

This allows the team to explain why a score changed between runs.

## 7. Recommended Initial Data Targets

Use these as calibration goals rather than absolute guarantees:

| Measure | Initial target |
| --- | ---: |
| Valid raw articles within 180 days | 1,000+ |
| Active sources | 50–70 |
| Independent ownership groups | 35–50 |
| Source categories | At least 7 |
| Vendor share | Preferably below 20% |
| Official/regulation/procurement share | At least 10–20% |
| Relevant articles after triage | Approximately 150–300 |
| Human-labelled triage validation sample | At least 100–200 |
| Evidence for a publishable topic | At least 3 independent records |
| Evidence for a high-confidence topic | At least 5 records across 3 signal types plus Orange evidence |

Coverage should also be checked per target vertical. A total of 1,000 articles is not sufficient if several priority verticals still have almost no usable evidence.

## 8. Recommended Integration Plan

### Priority 1 — Preserve the existing application foundation

Reuse from the DemoTest branch:

- Streamlit interface;
- SQLite and run history;
- source management;
- API client, limits, retries, and batching;
- company document extraction;
- evidence URL storage;
- deterministic scoring utilities;
- Radar/Watchlist UI concepts.

### Priority 2 — Replace the one-step analysis with the two-stage prompts

Implement:

1. Stage 1 triage using `prompt_triage_rss_orange_radar.md`;
2. persisted evidence extraction;
3. event deduplication;
4. Stage 2 clustering and scoring using `prompt_scoring_orange_radar.md`.

Add `link` to the Stage 1 output schema before implementation.

### Priority 3 — Merge the richer source registry

Use `source_registry.csv` and `source_backlog.csv` concepts in the application database, including independence group and source quality.

### Priority 4 — Build a balanced 180-day corpus

Target 1,000+ valid raw records, but monitor source category, ownership, domain, vertical, date, and full-text coverage continuously.

### Priority 5 — Make scoring evidence-driven

Move source diversity, specificity, recency, evidence thresholds, and final totals into deterministic Python. Mark momentum as unavailable until a valid baseline exists.

### Priority 6 — Connect Orange right-to-win evidence

Import partner activity, offering, customer-reference, and capability records into structured tables and calculate right to win independently from RSS attractiveness.

### Priority 7 — Connect the visualization to generated data

Replace hard-coded JavaScript opportunity objects with generated JSON or database-backed data. Add evidence URLs, score arithmetic, and coverage indicators.

### Priority 8 — Validate with humans

Before calling the product an MVP, run a reviewed evaluation sample and document:

- triage performance;
- clustering errors;
- duplicate-event errors;
- score-calculation accuracy;
- source coverage gaps;
- stakeholder acceptance of at least several topic cards.

## 9. Practical MVP Definition

The MVP does not need a perfect automated radar. It should demonstrate one complete, trustworthy loop:

1. collect a balanced 180-day evidence corpus;
2. classify articles with reviewed Stage 1 triage;
3. extract traceable evidence records;
4. deduplicate repeated events;
5. cluster evidence into specific opportunity spaces;
6. calculate reproducible scores;
7. separate attractiveness from right to win;
8. keep weak topics on the watchlist;
9. publish several reviewed topic cards;
10. display them dynamically in the radar interface.

A credible MVP result could contain only three to five reviewed opportunity spaces. Trustworthiness, traceability, and clear evidence gaps matter more than producing a large number of visually impressive but unsupported bubbles.

---

## 10. Final Team Recommendation

The team already has three strong layers:

1. a clear opportunity-space and evidence methodology;
2. detailed triage and clustering/scoring prompts plus a compelling watchlist visualization;
3. a functional application foundation with persistence, batching, controls, and UI.

The largest remaining task is to connect these layers correctly.

The recommended direction is:

`DemoTest application foundation + two-stage agent prompts + controlled evidence model + deterministic aggregate scoring + dynamic visualization`

The 1,000-article target is useful for calibration, but evidence diversity, event independence, source quality, and Orange right-to-win proof should determine whether an opportunity is publishable.
