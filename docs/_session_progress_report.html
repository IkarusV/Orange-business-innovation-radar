SECTION 1

Executive summary

Where the application stands now, against the exact gaps the baseline document flagged.

The baseline report's single biggest gap — "the scoring model does not explain itself, and the number it produces is not an attractiveness score" — is closed. The old "Strategic fit" figure (article count ÷ largest space's article count) is gone. In its place is a five-component, weighted Attractiveness score with every component visible, and a fully independent Now/Next/Later timing rule that no longer reads that score at all — enforced by a dedicated test, not just a comment.

Of the baseline's 8 MISSING requirements, all 8 are now MET. Of its 6 PARTIAL requirements, 4 are now MET; the remaining 2 (freshness display, value-driver tagging) were not touched today and are exactly as they were this morning — not attempted, not regressed. The three-way open question the baseline flagged — persona, geography and business-domain filtering, "your call, not a coding decision" — was answered by building all three.

None of this was free, and it should not be presented as finished — three things are worth saying up front, not discovered later:

  • Right-to-win / CRM data still does not exist anywhere in the app. The new "why this matters" field and the Presales sort both have real, tested code paths waiting for it — today they honestly say so instead of inventing a number.
  • The new Next/Later independence is real but the corpus is thin. 2 of 21 spaces land in "Next" today (9.5%) — the rule works, the live evidence base just isn't deep enough yet for more of it to show. Section 3 shows why with a real example.
  • A handful of countries — Greece foremost — don't yet resolve to a geography region. They are reported as gaps, not silently misfiled into the wrong bucket. See Section 4.

SECTION 3

Deep dive: the Attractiveness score, now real

What the baseline report's Section 3 showed as broken, and what replaced it.

SPECIFIED (slide 17, illustrative weights)

  • 30% Market signal strength — how visible across external sources
  • 20% Source diversity — independent-source coverage
  • 20% Evidence quality — is the signal relevant and credible
  • 15% Novelty and momentum — is the topic emerging or rising
  • 15% Strategic relevance — does it fit Orange Business

IMPLEMENTED TODAY (services/attractiveness.py)

  • 30% Market signal strength — recency-decayed evidence density, ~9-month half-life, normalised against the strongest space this run
  • 20% Source credibility — mean trust score from a 9-tier publisher-category anchor table (renamed from "diversity"; rejected as the wrong signal given how sources are found)
  • 20% Evidence quality — mean classifier confidence, now under an anchored rubric instead of an unanchored self-report
  • 15% Novelty & momentum — percentile rank of recent-vs-prior growth against every other space this run, not a fixed curve (most spaces have 1–2 articles; a fixed curve was pure noise)
  • 15% Strategic relevance — match against priorities set on the Company page
combined_score = Σ(weight × value) / Σ(weight of available components)
  — components with no data are excluded, remaining weights rescale, never counted as zero

One bug found and fixed along the way

seed_hardcoded_sources()'s upsert set the trust category but not the audited-at timestamp, so TED and CORDIS — the two highest-trust feeds, backing 18 of 34 classifications — stayed flagged "unaudited" and silently zeroed the Source credibility component on almost every space. Found and fixed before this report was written; verified against live data afterward.

The horizon fix

The baseline report's chart showed "Next" was structurally unreachable. Here is the same chart, and why it's still thin rather than declared solved.

This morning: horizon = "Now" if relevance >= 82 else "Next" if relevance >= 68 else "Later"
  — a threshold cut on the same volume-proxy score. Largest space held 2 articles, so relevance could only be 50 or 100. Nothing could ever land between.

Now: horizon = f(signal_type, signal_date, event_date, source_count) — never reads the score.
  — asserted by a test: a high-volume space evidenced only by tech_maturity signals must return Later; a low-volume space with one recent tender must return Next.
This morning

Now
2 / 21spaces genuinely in "Next" (9.5%)

Why still only 2, honestly

25 of the 34 live classified signals are CORDIS tech_maturity records — funded research that hasn't reached a buyer yet, which correctly lands in "Later". The rule is not the bottleneck; the live corpus' composition is. One of the two "Next" spaces:

Healthcare · Diagnostic support × Machine Learning — 1 proof signal + 1 tech-maturity signal, but only 1 of 2 sources and 1 of 2 concrete signals needed for "Now" — the interface literally states: "concrete evidence exists but does not converge: only 1 of 2 concrete signals, only 1 of 2 distinct sources." That sentence is the rule explaining itself on a real space, not a summary written after the fact.

Recommendation: re-measure this distribution after the next real collection run rather than loosening the convergence thresholds now — narrowing them to force more "Next" results would defeat the reason this was rebuilt.

Three new dimensions, and their honest gaps

All three were MISSING in the baseline report. Each is real, tested, and each has a reported — not hidden — limitation.

Business domain

6 domains, derived deterministically as the union of a space's technology-domain mapping and its use-case-domain mapping (technology always wins for the single-value "primary" badge). Live distribution: OX: Smart Industries 11, Cloud 6, CX 2, Cybersecurity 2. No domain fell under the 5% coverage floor. Pipelineteamfile/common/business_domains.py

Target persona

8 buyer personas (CIO, CISO, CDO, COO, CMO/CX, IT & Network, Quality Manager, Industrial Safety Manager), weighted Primary/Secondary/Peripheral rather than boolean — the weight is what makes ranking possible, not just filtering. All 21 live spaces resolve at least one persona. A persona × vertical suppression list zeroes out implausible pairs (e.g. Industrial Safety Manager × Retail). Pipelineteamfile/common/personas.py

Gap: persona selection is currently a single-select picker inside Sales mode, not a standalone multi-select chip filter like domain and geography — same underlying capability, different UI treatment. Worth unifying.

Geography

14 regions matching how the business actually segments Europe (France and Germany each standalone, DACH meaning Switzerland+Austria only — deliberate, not a mistake to "fix"). Deterministic for TED/CORDIS/OCDS, LLM-inferred for RSS. 16 of 21 spaces resolve a region; 5 render honestly as "Global / unspecified" rather than being excluded. Pipelineteamfile/common/geography.py

Reported gap, not silently misfiled: Greece appears in 12 of the 21 live spaces and has no home in the region table — it doesn't fall under Southern Europe (Italy/Spain/Portugal/Israel only), and the code deliberately refuses to push it into "Asia" via a naive continent fallback or bury it in "Global". It returns unresolved and is flagged. Cyprus, Malta, Turkey, Russia, the Western Balkans, Moldova and Belarus sit in the same hole. Fix is a one-line taxonomy edit plus a backfill rerun, once someone decides where Greece belongs.

SECTION 5

A new workflow layer: Role Mode

Not in the baseline scorecard at all — a new capability, not a gap fix.

Three view modes — Strategist, Sales, Presales — implemented as a pure view configuration, never written to the opportunity record. Switching mode changes three things at once: the default filters (Sales asks for a persona before showing results), the sort key (Sales sorts by a persona-weighted score — base score × a dampened multiplier that reorders without letting a weak-fit topic outrank a strong one), and which parts of the detail page lead, sit standard, or start collapsed. No region is ever fully hidden in any mode — asserted by a test rendering all three modes and checking every region is still in the page.

Mode only re-seeds filters if the user hasn't touched them — changing a filter is a normal action, never silently discarded by switching mode. Sales mode also switches the list view from a card grid to one wide card per topic, since the brief describes it being used live in front of a customer. services/role_modes.py · config/role_modes.json

Deterministic explanation fields

The baseline report's "Recommended move" — a single hardcoded constant, identical on every topic — is replaced by three composed fields built from typed clauses, not sentence templates with slots:

  • Why hot now — one clause per recent qualifying signal, capped at 3, strongest signal type first.
  • Why this matters — always exactly 2 clauses: a domain/vertical framing sentence, then a right-to-win sentence (today, always the honest fallback — see Section 6).
  • Recommended move — a 9-cell role-mode × horizon matrix plus an action clause keyed to the dominant signal type; genuinely different text per mode for the same topic, closing the exact bug the baseline report called out by file and line number.

No structured "entity" field exists on a signal record (only free-text rationale) — clauses lean on a type lead-in plus a truncated rationale rather than guessing at extraction. Deliberately rejected using the article's source name as a stand-in actor (e.g. "Defense Daily committed...") since that names the publisher, not the actor, which would read as confidently false.
