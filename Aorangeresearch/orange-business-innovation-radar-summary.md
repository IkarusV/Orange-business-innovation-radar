# Orange Business Innovation Radar: Research and Scoring Summary

## Purpose

Create a living, explainable innovation radar for Orange Business. The radar should surface specific opportunity spaces, rather than broad technology themes, and help strategists, sales teams, and presales teams decide what to do next.

An opportunity space is defined as:

`Vertical x Use Case x Technology`

Example: `Banking x secure WAN visibility x network security analytics`.

## Core Principle

The radar must not return an unexplained number. Every ranked topic must show:

- Attractiveness score and factor breakdown
- Urgency / time horizon
- Dated source evidence
- Signal type
- Why hot now
- Why it matters to Orange Business and the target customer
- Right-to-win evidence
- Recommended next action

If a user cannot explain why a topic is ranked, the score is not good enough.

## Strategic Context for Orange Business

Orange's `Trust the future` strategy prioritizes trusted digital services and identifies Orange Business and Orange Cyberdefense as growth vehicles for:

- Cyberdefense
- Trusted cloud solutions
- Trusted AI services
- Secure connectivity
- Selected verticals, notably defense and health

The Group aims to add EUR1bn of revenue by 2028 from fast-growing services versus 2025. Of this, EUR500m is expected from trusted B2B services. Orange Cyberdefense has a EUR2bn revenue objective for 2030.

In H1 2026, Orange Business reported:

- Revenue of EUR3.503bn, down 3.1% on a comparable basis
- EBITDAaL of EUR232m, down 6.4% on a comparable basis
- Fixed-only services down 8.9%, reflecting declines in legacy data and voice services
- IT and integration services up 1.3%, primarily supported by cybersecurity
- Orange Cyberdefense revenue of EUR645m, up 10.6% year-on-year

### Interpretation

Orange Business is transitioning away from legacy connectivity revenue. The radar should favor opportunities that strengthen growth portfolios: cybersecurity, trusted cloud, trusted AI, secure connectivity, and vertical solutions.

## Scoring Model

Keep opportunity attractiveness separate from Orange Business's right to win.

### 1. Opportunity Attractiveness

The initial model in the Innovation Radar briefing is:

`Attractiveness = 30% market signal strength + 20% source diversity + 20% evidence quality + 15% novelty/momentum + 15% Orange Business strategic relevance`

| Factor | What it measures | Evidence examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market signal strength | Visibility and magnitude of demand | Market growth, tender volume, customer investment, adoption data |
| Source diversity | Whether evidence is independent and repeated | Regulation, analyst research, customer news, procurement data, industry media |
| Evidence quality | Credibility and specificity | Official regulation, named customer commitment, reputable analyst data, quantified results |
| Novelty and momentum | Whether the topic is rising or changing | New deadlines, rapid adoption, recent production deployments, new product maturity |
| Strategic relevance | Fit with Orange Business priorities | Cybersecurity, cloud, AI, connectivity, CX, EX, Smart Industries, target verticals |

### 2. Right to Win

Score separately once Orange-owned evidence is collected:

`Right to Win = 30% offering/asset fit + 25% customer overlap + 25% reference cases + 20% skills and partner readiness`

| Factor | Evidence examples |
| --- | --- |
| Offering / asset fit | Existing Orange offers, platforms, infrastructure, cyber or cloud capability |
| Customer overlap | Existing accounts in the target vertical or geography |
| Reference cases | Delivered solutions, pilots, contracts, measurable outcomes |
| Skills and partner readiness | Delivery teams, certified expertise, technology partners, implementation capacity |

### 3. Confidence

Publish a confidence label alongside scores. Confidence depends on the number, quality, recency, and independence of cited sources.

- High: multiple recent, independent, credible sources and Orange evidence
- Medium: enough evidence to test the topic, but gaps remain
- Low: early signal, vendor-led claim, or single-source evidence

## Urgency and Time Horizon

Urgency is independent from attractiveness.

| Horizon | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Now | Active regulation, imminent compliance deadline, live tenders, named buyer activity, or production-ready technology with clear demand |
| Next | Clear market momentum and a likely demand window within 12-24 months |
| Later / Watchlist | Credible early signal, but limited buyer trigger, immature technology, or insufficient evidence |

## Signal Taxonomy

Each source item should be tagged with one signal type:

- Regulation: law, policy, standard, compliance deadline
- Buying signal: tender, budget announcement, procurement activity, customer task force
- Market trend: market growth, adoption statistics, analyst forecast
- Market move: acquisition, investment, partnership, competitor action
- Technology maturity: production readiness, benchmark, certification, capability threshold
- Proof signal: named pilot, deployment, contract, measurable result

## Proposed Research Workflow

### Phase 1: Map Orange Business's Right to Win

1. Build a shortlist of 20 major customers and partners.
2. Classify each organisation by:
   - Organisation type: customer, technology partner, ecosystem partner, delivery partner
   - Tier: strategic/global, key regional, reference customer, emerging
   - Vertical, geography, and Orange Business domain
3. Collect Orange Business case studies, press releases, blogs, partner pages, and announcements.
4. Extract structured evidence from each source:
   - Organisation
   - Vertical
   - Use case
   - Technology
   - Orange offering/domain
   - Outcome or proof point
   - Publication date
   - Source URL
5. Identify repeated `Vertical x Use Case x Technology` combinations.

This phase establishes Orange Business's right to play and potential to win. It is especially useful for references, customer access, offerings, partners, and recommended account actions.

### Phase 2: Validate Market Attractiveness and Urgency

For each candidate opportunity space, gather independent evidence from:

- Official regulations and standards bodies
- Customer announcements, tenders, and procurement portals
- Analyst research and market reports
- Credible industry news
- Technology providers only as supplementary, clearly labelled evidence

Use this evidence to validate market demand, urgency, source diversity, and technology maturity. Orange-owned blogs and case studies alone should not be used to establish market attractiveness.

### Phase 3: Rank and Act

For every publishable topic:

1. Calculate attractiveness, right-to-win, urgency, and confidence.
2. Present the evidence and explanations in the topic card.
3. Assign a practical action:
   - Sales opener for a target account or vertical
   - Customer workshop
   - Internal discovery or technical deep-dive
   - Partner assessment
   - Watchlist and scheduled refresh

## How to Use the Results

| Situation | Recommended decision |
| --- | --- |
| High attractiveness + high right to win | Prioritize as an innovation investment and commercial play |
| High attractiveness + weak right to win | Keep as a strategic watchlist topic; assess partners, offerings, and capability gaps |
| Moderate attractiveness + strong right to win | Use for account expansion, reference-led selling, and targeted campaigns |
| Low evidence confidence | Do not publish as a ranked opportunity; retain in the watchlist and gather evidence |
